    Hear and see how the factory is running

    This is a simulated factory day: materials arrive, three assembly lines build products, QA inspects them, and trucks collect them from the warehouse.

    Rather than reading numbers, let your own pattern recognition do the work. After a while you'll know what a normal day looks and sounds like, and notice when something changes.

    • Listen. Roughly harmonious, flowing music means things are fine. Discordant, off-key or off-beat sound means something isn't quite right.
    • Look. Each coloured thread is one measurement; wider means more. Newest is at the top. Red dots are alarms.
    • Read the harmony. Flow harmony in the top bar sums it up: 1.00 means everything flows, and it falls as lines run short of materials, trucks queue up or QA rejects rise.
    • Break things. Use Cause a disruption on the right, change event rates, and switch homeostasis off to see whether the factory recovers on its own.
    • Compare. Open the CCTV panel at the bottom left to watch cartoon cameras of the factory floor, with alarms flashing as they happen. Try the same disruption both ways: watching the cameras, then just listening and following the chart and event log. Which tells you sooner that something's wrong, and what it is?

    Flow harmony is a single score for how well the factory is flowing, from 1.00 (everything moving) down to 0.00 (badly stuck).

    It falls when lines run short of materials, trucks queue in the yard, QA rejects rise or a line stops. It recovers gradually, so a dip lingers for a few minutes after the cause has passed.

    The word gives the band: smooth at 0.75 and above, strained from 0.45, struggling below that. The music follows it: the lower the score, the more off-key and off-beat it sounds.
