    A factory day you can hear

    This is a simulated factory. Materials arrive, three assembly lines build products, QA inspects them and trucks carry them away. Instead of reading numbers, you'll learn what a normal day sounds and looks like, and you'll notice when it changes, often before you could say why.

    Listen
    Calm, in-key music means the factory is flowing. When it drifts off key or off the beat, something isn't right.
    Look
    Each coloured thread is one measurement, oldest at the top and growing down to a white line marking now. Wider means more; red dots are alarms.
    Read the harmony
    The score in the top bar sums it up, from 1.00 when everything flows to 0.00 when it's stuck. The line beneath names the likely cause.

    Flow harmony is a single score for how well the factory is flowing, from 1.00 (everything moving) down to 0.00 (badly stuck).

    It falls when lines run short of materials, trucks queue in the yard, QA rejects rise or a line stops. It recovers gradually, so a dip lingers for a few minutes after the cause has passed.

    The word gives the band: smooth at 0.75 and above, strained from 0.45, struggling below that. The music follows it: the lower the score, the more off-key and off-beat it sounds.
